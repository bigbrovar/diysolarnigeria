*From posts by bigbrovar, Samil. Discussed in the group: August 2023.*

**Which way should panels face in Nigeria, and at what angle?**

## Face south, lie nearly flat

bigbrovar's guidance:

- Nigeria is north of the equator, so the sun travels from east to west along an arc to the south. Panels should face south as much as possible.
- Tilt matters more than direction. Aim for about 5°. Anything from 0° (flat) to 15°, tilted towards the south, is very good.
- A flat panel faces no direction at all, and it produces about what a south-facing panel at 5° would.
- If you cannot face due south, use the direction closest to south.

## Check before the installer mounts anything

Samil's advice is to settle this before installation, not after:

1. Find out which way your house and roof faces. A compass app is enough.
2. Work out the tilt your location needs.
3. Compare that with the slope of your roof, and adjust the mounting to make up the difference.

A poor installation leaves solar output on the table for the life of the system. If an installer tells you raised or adjusted mounting "won't be firm", get a second opinion.

## When the roof does not face south

bigbrovar's own roof has no south face. It faces south-east (124° azimuth) and north-west (305° azimuth). His simulated results:

| Array | Direction | Output compared with an ideal south-facing install |
|---|---|---|
| 5.4 kW main array | South-east | About 4% less |
| 2.2 kW second array | North-west | About 15% less |

He keeps both on purpose. In rainy season the morning can be cloudy while the late afternoon is sunny, so the west-facing array covers for the east-facing one. The main array meets his daily need and the second is spare capacity for cloudy periods.

## A tool for the job

He recommends the Android app [Solar Power Pro](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.exigo.solarpowerpro). It has a compass for direction and an inclinometer for tilt. The paid version can simulate the expected yield for each direction.

## Tilt and dust

*This section is from published sources, not from group posts.*

Panel makers' installation manuals add one consideration that matters in harmattan: dust.

- Several manufacturers, including SoliTek and Chint (Astronergy), recommend a tilt of at least 10°, so that rain can wash dust off the glass.
- Q CELLS allows as little as 3°, but tells installers to check regularly for dirt build-up on any array tilted at less than 20°.

This does not contradict the advice above. A tilt between 5° and 15° gives the best output for the sun's position. Towards the flat end of that range, expect to clean the panels yourself more often. Towards 10° to 15°, rain does more of it for you. Check the minimum tilt in your own panel's installation manual, since it can be a warranty condition.

Sources: [SoliTek installation manual](https://share.finqu.com/1043d82eee4cb7e0978dbb8cc429a67f51b598b586a7222a593c1cf6553b99c6/solitek-solid-installation-manual.pdf), [Astronergy (Chint Solar) module manual](https://manuals.online/astronergy/solar-panel/chsm6610m/user-manual/18e4a), [Q CELLS installation manual](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1340059/Q-Cells-Q-Pro.html?page=9).
